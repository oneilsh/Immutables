Benchmarking flexseq Against Base R and rstackdeque

Method

This article adapts the operation-by-operation style used in Barry (2018), while narrowing the comparison set to flexseq, base R lists, and rstackdeque.

  • Each timing cell builds a fresh object of size n, then times one operation.
  • Sequence timings compare append, middle replace, middle get, and middle remove.
  • Queue timings compare FIFO enqueue at the back and dequeue from the front.
  • The reduced article grid uses sizes 100, 500, 1000, 5000, 10000, 50000 with 10 repeats per cell.
  • Middle reads use [[ for both flexseq and base lists; middle removal times the updated sequence (pop_at(... )$remaining for flexseq).

Notes

  • flexseq sequence updates stay competitive while keeping persistence semantics explicit.
  • Queue timings use the same FIFO direction across implementations: push_back()/pop_front() for flexseq, insert_back()/without_front() for rstackdeque, and append/front-removal for base lists.
  • These figures are meant for article exposition rather than internal regression tracking.
